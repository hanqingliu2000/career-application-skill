# Employer and job research

Establish the user's target geography, role interests, constraints and research scope from their instructions and evidence; do not assume these. For an employer list, apply criteria the user provides and explain why each is relevant, without treating reputation alone as proof of opportunity or fit. Distinguish organization-level interest from whether a particular team or role matches. Respect the requested breadth and the existing list; do not automatically expand it or change past decisions.

## Locate target roles and employers

When the user asks for career direction or is unsure what to target, derive options from their evidence rather than requiring a settled job title upfront. Start with demonstrated strengths: the problems solved, substantive tasks, business settings, deliverables, ownership and complexity. Combine these with the user's stated priorities and constraints, such as work interests, compensation, development, geography, language and work authorization. Ask only about unresolved tradeoffs that would materially change the options; leave unanswered preferences unknown.

Propose a manageable set of role families with typical responsibilities, likely title variants, supporting experience, material gaps and tradeoffs against the user's goals. Treat them as hypotheses, not confirmed preferences. Map promising families to employer types and relevant teams, then a focused employer shortlist; explain where the user's capabilities could create value. Keep employer appeal, role fit and hiring availability separate. Do not promise compensation or prospects from brand alone, or let prestige outweigh mandatory qualifications.

Within the authorized research scope, calibrate the hypotheses against a small, varied sample of current official JDs using the experience assessment below. Show what actual core work, seniority and mandatory requirements support or weaken each direction, with sources and check dates; disclose inaccessible or incomplete evidence. Use the user's reactions to the work and tradeoffs to narrow priorities, retaining the distinction between their decisions and the agent's recommendations. Update an existing plan or tracker only as authorized. If the user already has clear targets, use them directly rather than restarting discovery; do not turn direction exploration into an unrequested exhaustive vacancy search.

## Research current opportunities

For requested current vacancies, use official employer careers pages and linked applicant tracking systems first, with third-party sources as discovery or corroboration. Match the depth of collection to the request: a specified role, sample or keyword search does not authorize an exhaustive employer search. Apply the following coverage checks when researching a broader set of vacancies, within the user's chosen employers, locations, business areas and hiring categories.

### Establish channels and collect the actual listings

- Identify the relevant official channels before collecting vacancies: a central site, regional sites and important separately recruiting entities or platforms already within scope. Verify which scope each covers; do not assume one substitutes for another or enumerate every affiliated company. Recheck redirects and whether the destination is a current job list, an informational page or a different hiring category. A newly discovered site does not resolve coverage if it serves a different population or historical campaign.
- Within the agreed scope, prefer listings without a keyword filter so unfamiliar titles and synonyms remain discoverable; use keyword searches to supplement collection. Do not remove the user's deliberate restrictions. If full collection is impractical or unavailable, record the actual filters and extent examined and describe the result as partial. Counts shown by a region filter or search summary are discovery clues, not evidence that the actual records were retrieved.
- Follow pagination, load-more controls or equivalent listing navigation. Retain official job identifiers where available; otherwise use canonical links and corroborating fields without forcing ambiguous records together. Compare page counts, displayed totals, collected records and unique jobs where available. Explain duplicates, overlapping filters or changes during collection; record unresolved discrepancies rather than claiming completion. Dynamic listings may change during a run, so record collection times and avoid inventing exact reconciliation when the site cannot support it.

### Verify loading and support the completion claim

Confirm that a dynamic listing has finished loading before interpreting it. An empty shell, transient no-results message or access failure does not establish zero vacancies. When needed, follow the employer's current navigation, try another official entry or render the page in an available browser. Respect access controls; if loading or access remains unresolved, mark that channel incomplete rather than empty.

Keep a compact coverage record suited to the task: channel and actual destination, scope and filters, check time with timezone, pagination or collection extent, available totals and deduplication results, and supporting public evidence. Distinguish complete checks, partial or inaccessible channels, and user-authorized omissions. Historical records help navigation and comparison; they do not prove current availability. A research cycle may span days: retain each channel's actual check time and disclose the cycle's date range. Do not combine unrechecked historical lists into a claim of a fresh complete update.

Claim complete coverage only when all required channels in the agreed scope have been checked and material pagination or count discrepancies resolved or bounded transparently. Report remaining gaps and any authorized exclusions separately; preserve useful verified results when a channel remains blocked. Do not turn a partial search into a complete one by narrowing its scope after the fact.

### Read and assess candidate roles

Read the full job description before assessing a candidate position. Capture official employer and role identifier, title, location, core work, mandatory versus preferred qualifications, level, contract if stated, deadline, and direct description and application URLs. Check the live status and actual deadline using the stated timezone; treat an unspecified deadline, an ambiguous cutoff or a broken application link as unknown rather than closed. Separate posting, refresh, deadline and verification dates. A readable old posting, login page or apply button does not prove submission is possible. Check current application instructions for resume and other requested material language separately; posting or interface language alone is not an official requirement.

Evaluate actual daily responsibilities and outputs against documented direct experience and transferable capabilities. Assess seniority, total and role-specific experience separately, and identify mandatory qualifications, work authorization, language or other material gaps without guessing. Distinguish a reasonable stretch from a hard mismatch and a missing fact; do not turn keywords or employer brand into evidence of suitability. Explain supporting evidence, limitations and uncertainty, and leave the application decision to the user. If the user names a specific role, assess it even if weak or expired and clearly label its status and missing information; do not expand a single role into a broad search without authorization.

## Assess vertical experience

Start from the JD's core business setting, substantive professional tasks, expected deliverables and depth of responsibility. Separate domain knowledge from the work itself and from tools or methods. For each major requirement, identify the strongest evidence and classify it as direct relevant experience, closely comparable experience in another setting, general transferable ability, or not demonstrated in the available evidence. Explain the business-context, delivery-depth or ownership gap. A different industry does not by itself make experience irrelevant; shared tools, keywords, employer prestige or impressive metrics do not establish experience doing the core work.

Assess total tenure and relevant specialist experience separately, using known dates without rounding upward. Distinguish mandatory from preferred requirements and stated facts from reasonable inference; do not invent specialist credentials or management scope from a title. Distinguish a substantive experience gap from a presentation/evidence gap: better wording can clarify true relevant work but cannot supply missing experience. Missing evidence means not demonstrated, not proof of inability. Summarize the strongest matches and consequential gaps in a compact requirement-to-evidence comparison when helpful, without unsupported numerical fit scores. Leave the application decision to the user.

## Maintain the tracker

If tracker updates are requested or authorized, read and preserve its structure and user edits. Deduplicate using employer and official job identifier, then corroborate identity with links, title, team and location; preserve ambiguous conflicts. Record source, check date, fit and availability separately. Do not overwrite application decisions, submitted materials or history, infer an application from research, or mark a role applied when only a resume was prepared. Report coverage, results and unresolved access limitations.
